# R4 round 1 (a) — rule-based selection vs in-sample selection

Date: 2026-10-23. Control: R3 round 1 (0.0005, in-sample selected, OVERFIT).
Treatment: spec 0a84... (0.00625 = median of the grid, rule-selected, registered before the run).

Data: same tape (24,237 SPY 5-min bars, 2026-04-10 .. 2026-10-09).

## Results

| | selection method | threshold | verdict | oos return after cost | trades |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| control | in-sample (best OOS) | 0.0005 | OVERFIT | +0.140% | 144 |
| treatment (a) | rule (median of grid) | 0.00625 | INSUFFICIENT | +0.067% | 32 |

Cumulative trials: 180 (was 178). PASS: 0.

## Interpretation

The treatment could not be judged (32 trades -> INSUFFICIENT), so the "less overfit"
claim is untestable on this data. The rule-selected threshold produced fewer trades
than the in-sample one, not more, and neither SURVIVES.

Combined with R3 round 1 and R4 (0.005): on this data, the only threshold with enough
sample to judge (0.0005, 144 trades) overfits, and every wider threshold is
INSUFFICIENT. The edge, if it exists, is not visible at this horizon and cost.

## What this does not prove

- It does not prove no edge exists. It proves this search space, on this window, at this
  cost, with this candidate set, produced 0 SURVIVES in 180 trials.
- It does not resolve whether the overfit came from selection or from the rule itself,
  because the treatment could not be judged.
